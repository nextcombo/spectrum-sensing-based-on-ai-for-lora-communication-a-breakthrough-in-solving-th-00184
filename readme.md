# Spectrum Sensing Based on AI for LoRa Communication: A Breakthrough in Solving the Challenges of IoT Spectrum Resources with a Data Set

Title: AI-based LoRa communication spectrum perception technology, solving the IoT spectrum resource issue
Abstract: The core of the Internet of Things (IoT) ecosystem relies on wireless sensor networks and low-power wide area network technologies (such as LoRa modulation), enabling long-range, high-energy efficiency communication in multiple fields such as smart homes, healthcare, agriculture, etc. By 2034, it is estimated that IoT devices will reach about 41 billion, making spectrum resource management a critical issue. The limited spectrum resources brought about by the surge in devices have led to issues such as increased interference, packet collisions, and a decline in service quality. While existing methods for enhancing network capacity have significant limitations, there is an urgent need for advanced solutions.
This paper proposes a novel hybrid spectrum perception framework that integrates traditional signal processing with artificial intelligence technology, specifically designed for LoRa spread factor detection and communication channel analysis. This framework directly processes broadband signals from orthogonal quadrature (IQ) samples, achieving identification and classification of multiple concurrent LoRa transmission signals. Test results show that the framework performs exceptionally well, with detection accuracy reaching 96.2%, precision rate at 99.16%, and recall rate at 95.4%. Its flexible architecture separates the artificial intelligence processing pipeline from the channel analysis pipeline, ensuring compatibility with various communication protocols beyond LoRa.
Introduction: The Internet of Things connects the physical world to the internet through devices equipped with sensors and actuators, facilitating data collection, interaction, and transmission, fundamentally transforming multiple industries and daily life. Wireless sensor networks, leveraging low-power wide area network technologies, become the core support of the IoT ecosystem. This technology enables long-range, high-energy efficiency data transmission between gateways and convergence nodes, widely applied in smart homes, healthcare, precision agriculture, smart grids, transportation, and other areas.
Due to the diversification of application scenarios, the Internet of Things (IoT) market has seen significant growth, with an annual increase of about 2 billion devices, projected to reach 41 billion by 2034. Most low-power wide-area network technologies utilize unlicensed spectrum bands (868MHz, 915MHz, 2.4GHz), but these bands are bandwidth-limited, with LoRa accounting for approximately 40% of global IoT connections and being the most widely used technology. The extensive deployment of LoRa devices, coupled with limited spectrum resources, has led to increased packet collision rates, interference, and reduced network capacity, resulting in a decline in service quality. While existing methods for scaling such as duty cycle adjustment and redundancy mechanisms have not been effective in large-scale, high-density deployment scenarios.
Existing research has proposed improved modulation techniques such as dual-mode linear frequency-hopping spread spectrum (LFHSS) and frequency-sensing solutions, which can detect and identify wireless protocols within shared spectrum bands, aiding in dynamic frequency allocation and reducing interference. These advancements are crucial for enhancing the performance, reliability, and scalability of LoRa IoT networks. Advanced channel analysis can also provide network operators with insights into issues related to network scalability, while optimizing the allocation of spreading factors can mitigate capture effects and enhance network capacity—theoretically, LoRa spreading factors are orthogonal, allowing gateways to demodulate multiple signals from different spreading factors on the same carrier frequency. However, practical applications often encounter factors such as transmitter time jitter and carrier frequency offset that reduce the orthogonality, leading to interference between spreading factor pairs. Therefore, a rational strategy for spreading factor allocation is particularly important.
Network real-time monitoring tools based on frequency-sensing technology can track the allocation of spreading factors, identify sensor overuse of specific spreading factors, and optimize network performance based on the sensed results. They can also analyze network behavior deeply, enabling proactive interventions and troubleshooting.
Existing methods for enhancing LoRaWAN networks primarily rely on data packet metadata at the Media Access Control (MAC) layer, but they require first receiving and demodulating LoRa packets, and do not account for other communication protocols in the same frequency band. Therefore, network perception must be achieved through spectrum sensing technology at the physical link layer. Previous studies have utilized Convolutional Neural Networks to detect low-power wide area network protocols and classified LoRa spreading factors based on cyclic stationary features. The author's team has also used spectrum maps to detect LoRa spreading factors and simultaneously detect multiple signals, but these approaches all have certain limitations.
To this end, this paper proposes and evaluates a hybrid spectrum sensing framework that integrates traditional signal processing with artificial intelligence models. It directly implements LoRa spread factor detection, classification, and extraction of channel information such as center frequency and bandwidth from IQ samples. Its core feature is the separation of artificial intelligence from advanced analysis pipelines, enhancing the flexibility of the framework. This paper also elaborates on the design, implementation, dataset construction, model training, and practical deployment test results of this framework.
Method Introduction: The proposed hybrid spectrum perception framework is specifically designed for LoRa modulation spread factor detection and advanced channel analysis. Its core lies in the integration of traditional signal processing with artificial intelligence technologies driven by convolutional neural networks, directly processing IQ sample streams collected by software-defined radio devices to detect, classify, and analyze multiple concurrent LoRa transmission signals. The framework is divided into three core modules: transmission detection and clustering algorithms, a convolutional neural network for LoRa spread factor detection, and a pipeline for channel analysis processing. Each module operates independently but collaboratively, enhancing the framework's universality.
1  
Transfer Detection and Clustering Algorithm (TDCA)
Firstly, normalize the IQ samples to reduce computational overhead and enhance model performance. Then, convert the signals into the frequency domain through discrete Fourier transform (DFT). After spectral shifting, power spectral density calculation, and moving average denoising, employ a class energy detection method to identify the frequency bins occupied by the signal. By calculating the difference in index between adjacent frequency bins and comparing with a threshold, clustering is achieved. After removing invalid small clusters, bandpass filters are designed for each cluster to separate single transmission signals. Subsequently, the signals are converted back to the time domain through an inverse discrete Fourier transform and moved to the baseband. Finally, multiple single-transmission signals are outputted, preparing for subsequent processing. This algorithm is based on the general principle of spectrum perception and can be adapted to other narrowband modulation technologies such as FSK and NB-IoT.
1  
LoRa Spreading Factor Detection Convolutional Neural Network Design and Training
A dataset containing SF7 to SF12 six-class spread factors was constructed. MATLAB was used to generate 26,000 IQ samples for each class, simulating channel impairments such as Rayleigh fading, Doppler shift, and clock offset. The dataset covers 26 signal-to-noise ratio conditions ranging from -20dB to 30dB, resulting in a public dataset of 156,000 signal samples. A one-dimensional convolutional neural network with a hierarchical structure was designed using 4096 IQ samples as input. Through multiple layers of convolution, batch normalization, activation, and pooling, features were extracted. Global average pooling, fully connected layers, and a Softmax layer were used to output the probability of spread factor categories. The network consists of 26 layers and 258,950 trainable parameters. A 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% test dataset split was adopted, with the model trained for 100 epochs using a random gradient descent with momentum optimizer. The model achieved rapid convergence, achieving 99.69% and 99.97% classification accuracy on both simulated datasets and indoor software defined radio measurements, respectively.
1  
Channel analysis processing pipeline
For each detected LoRa transmission signal, the center frequency is determined by the transmission detection and clustering algorithm, and the signal bandwidth is calculated using the 99% occupied bandwidth method. The occupied degree of the entire analysis band is calculated through a formula, quantifying the utilization of spectrum resources; this pipeline can output the center frequency, bandwidth, spreading factor, and overall occupied degree of each LoRa signal, providing data support for dynamic allocation of spectrum resources.
1. Real-world deployment test
A test environment consisting of an emitter with 5 LoRa Internet of Things nodes and a receiver equipped with software-defined radio was set up, with the transmitter and receiver being 50 meters apart and located in different buildings, generating 100 transmission frames (each frame containing 5 different channels and spreading factors of LoRa transmission signals). The performance of the framework was comprehensively evaluated based on accuracy, precision, recall, average center frequency deviation, and average bandwidth deviation as indicators to assess its performance in real scenarios.
Conclusion: This paper proposes and evaluates a novel hybrid spectrum perception framework specifically designed for LoRa spreading factor detection and communication channel analysis. The framework integrates traditional signal processing with artificial intelligence technology, directly detecting and classifying multiple concurrent LoRa transmission signals in broadband signals based on IQ samples. It performs exceptionally well in inference tests in real scenarios, achieving detection accuracy of 96.2%, precision rate of 99.16%, and recall rate of 95.4%, with an average center frequency deviation of only 9.13kHz and an average bandwidth deviation of 10.2kHz, demonstrating the framework's high precision and reliability.
The framework aims to address issues such as packet collisions, interference, and reduced channel capacity in large-scale, high-density LoRa IoT networks due to limited spectrum resources. It enables dynamic allocation of communication channels and precise analysis of channel states, effectively ensuring the performance, scalability, and reliability of IoT networks. Additionally, the framework separates the LoRa spreading factor detection pipeline from the channel analysis pipeline, forming a flexible architecture that can be adapted to various communication protocols other than LoRa, significantly enhancing its applicability across various IoT scenarios.
Furthermore, this framework also has certain limitations: Firstly, the transmission detection and clustering algorithms are based on traditional energy detection and rule-based clustering methods, which are prone to missed detection under noise or complex channel conditions; Secondly, convolutional neural networks currently have a fixed requirement of 1MHz sampling rate, limiting the scope of the framework.
Future research will address the limitations mentioned above: an advanced one-dimensional semantic segmentation neural network will be adopted in place of transmission detection and clustering algorithms, enabling it to learn and extract radio transmission signals based on cluster patterns under complex conditions. Training datasets with different sampling rates will be constructed, and convolutional neural networks will be optimized to directly process inputs at different sampling rates without the need for explicit resampling. Addressing these issues will significantly enhance the framework's generality, accuracy, and applicability in dynamic IoT environments, providing support for the sustainable development and robust operation of LoRa IoT networks.
